Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism area extended to Iceland and Norway
Iceland and Norway will become part of the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), following decision by the EEA Joint Committee, which entered into force on 18 November. This means that as of 1 January 2027, the mechanism will fully apply in Iceland and Norway goods imported into their customs territories, in the same way that it applies to goods entering the EU customs territory.
CBAM is a climate measure designed to prevent carbon leakage, support global decarbonisation, and ensure that imported goods are subject to the same carbon price faced by EU producers under the EU Emissions Trading System.
The extension follows the incorporation of the relevant EU legislation into the EEA Agreement. It ensures a more consistent application of carbon pricing rules across the EU, Iceland and Norway.
By extending the CBAM area to Iceland and Norway, the EU and its EEA partners are strengthening efforts to prevent carbon leakage and to maintain a level playing field for industry. The extension also reflects the shared commitment of the EU, Iceland and Norway towards ambitious decarbonisation, fair competition and greater transparency on the embedded emissions of imported goods.
For businesses, this means that goods imported from outside the enlarged CBAM area will face CBAM rules, regardless of whether they enter through the EU, Iceland or Norway. By contrast, EU goods exported to Iceland or Norway, as well as Icelandic and Norwegian goods imported into the EU, already do not incur additional CBAM charges.
The mechanism continues to cover the sectors already included in CBAM, such as iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen.
