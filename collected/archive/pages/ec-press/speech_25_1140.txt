[제목] Introductory remarks by Commissioner Hoekstra at the European Parliament's Committee on the Environment, Climate and Food Safety (ENVI)

Dear Chair,
Honourable Members,
It's great to be here today with all of you, and thank you very much for organising this Structured Dialogue with the ENVI Committee.
Since the start of the mandate, I've been fortunate to be in touch with many of you one-on-one, but I'm delighted to be here for an in-depth exchange with all of you formally on EU climate policies.
Dear Chair,
Dear Members,
I'll mention a few things that in my view are most likely to be top of mind, but I am sure we are going to discuss much more in the course of this debate.
We truly see unprecedented change to global geopolitics. We have entered a geopolitical winter, full frontal. That affects the domain of trade; the domain of safety and security; the domain of economics at large, but certainly also unfortunately the domain of international climate diplomacy.
The EU is facing many challenges at home, but certainly also abroad.
We are facing at home the conumdrum of needing to make sure that we continue with our climate policies, but making sure that our businesses become ever more competitive and that we actually decrease our dependencies on regimes not to be trusted from outside.
This is what is at the heart of the Clean Industrial Deal.
Having said all of this, it is extremely important to state once again that we can decide to ignore the problem of climate change for a couple of days, but then it will still be there.
This isn't a problem we can choose to address or ignore because the price tag – in human, economic and environmental terms – is only going up.
We should not be mistaken by the 1.5 degree watermark, because unfortunately it could well be the case that we will override it.
What is even more worrying for Europeans is that this is an average for the world at large.
For Europe, the number is 3 degrees. And that is of course not evenly distributed accross the Union. Some places in the EU will be even more on the receiving end of climate change.
The price tag will only go up.
This is why Europe will continue with climate action, within Europe and globally.
First, on the domestic front.
When it comes to 2030 targets, we're quite on track.
In the coming weeks, we'll be publishing the EU-wide assessment of National Energy and Climate Plans.
Despite some areas for improvement in specific categories for specific Member States, we're well on track to achieve our 55% reduction objective by 2030. That is good news.
There was a remarkable drop in emissions in 2023 – down from 8.3% the previous year, largely driven by reductions in ETS sectors, mainly the power sector and energy intensive industries.
Figures for the ETS sectors show this trend continued last year.
To put it into perspective, ETS emissions are now around 50% below 2005 levels and well on track to achieve the 2030 target of -62%.
Another important figure is that 47% of electricity came from renewable sources last year.
So we're making progress.
But it's clear there's more work to do.
We will only be sucessful if, ever more than before, we manage to bridge the domains of competitiveness, of climate action, and of independence.
Decarbonisation should be a reason for each of these domains.
That is what is at the heart of the Clean Industrial Deal.
And the focus on the one side on heavy industry and on the other side on the new kids on the block. Think about clean tech, high-growth type of companies that we want to have in the EU going forward.
It's a business plan to drive competitiveness, boost our strategic independence and accelerate climate action.
Another important part of our climate work is our comprehensive strategy to strengthen CBAM.
As we speak, European exporters often are at a disadvantage.
They pay for their carbon emissions while their competitors don't. This is unacceptable.
We have made a huge step forward in terms of CBAM simplification, but that is not enough.
In the remainder of the year, we seek to tackle what we call downstream products, circumventions and exports.
I have the ambition to tackle all these elements before the year is over.
This is tremendously important for creating a level-playing field, for making sure we do not see any carbon leakage and for making sure that it is fair and just for our companies.
I keep reminding everyone that the alternative is way worse.
Not just for our companies, for our people, but also for climate. If companies go bankrupt, we will have to import.
And you can be sure of one thing: the way things are produced outside of the EU are not as green as how it is produced inside the EU.
That is why it is so important to get this right.
This brings me to my work on the international front.
Later on this week, I will be at the Copenhagen Ministerial as part of the rythm that always preceeds the COP.
A few weeks ago, I travelled to Brazil to meet with Brazilian government leadership, NGOs and business leaders ahead of COP30 in Belém later this year. I can only commend their leadership so far.
The reality is of course that we are facing an even more difficult geopolitical landscape that has hugely affected the situation in the UAE and even more so at the last COP. That trend will continue.
It will take craftmenship, it will take a lot of efforts and bridgebuilding with our friends from the global South and our more traditional friends to make sure we deliver ambitious NDCs, but also concrete plans.
Carbon markets and carbon pricing are high on my list.
More efforts and more concreteness on mitigation is clearly on my list.
I will make sure I will keep you updated in the weeks and months going forward.
So, on that note, Chair, Honourable Members, I'll conclude my introductory remarks and look forward to our exchange.
