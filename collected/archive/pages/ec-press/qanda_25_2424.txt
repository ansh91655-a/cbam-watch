[제목] Questions and answers on the 2025 Overview Report on Simplification, Implementation and Enforcement of EU rules

What is the main goal of this report – and what does it include?
The European Commission is engaged in an unprecedented simplification effort to reduce the administrative burden and cost for businesses by €37.5 billion by the end of 2030. The 2025 Overview Report on Simplification, Implementation and Enforcement outlines concrete measures delivered so far, as well as what is on the table and the direction ahead.
The report presents:
the proposed omnibus initiatives, which aim to reduce burdens and simplify legislation;
the new tools and instruments that the Commission has put in place to speed up the simplification and implementation of EU laws, policies, and funding across Member States;
the steps the Commission has taken to ensure that EU law is enforced, e.g. through dialogues with Member States and infringement procedures.
This is the first annual overview report summarising the main results of the Commission's work towards a simpler and more agile Europe across three key components: simplification, implementation and enforcement. It covers the period from 1 January to 31 July 2025.
This report builds on the individual progress reports on simplification, implementation and enforcement.
Why is the Commission engaged in simplifying EU rules?
Simplification is an essential aspect of a necessary and ambitious EU competitiveness agenda. Over 60% of EU companies see regulation as an obstacle to investment, with 55% of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) flagging regulatory obstacles and administrative burdens as their greatest challenge.
The Draghi and Letta reports also highlight that administrative burdens and complexity of EU laws are limiting the EU's economic potential and prosperity.
Regular contacts with companies, experts and practitioners allow us to assess progress and identify simplification opportunities, key implementation challenges and gaps. That is why the Commission introduced implementation dialogues and ‘reality checks' with stakeholders and directly with companies to understand the impact of EU legislation on their daily business operations.
What simplification initiatives have been undertaken and what will their effect be?
Starting in February 2025, the Commission has put forward proposals that aim to reduce administrative burden and simplify the EU legislative framework.
Proposed omnibus and simplification initiatives aim to bring more than €8.6 billion in annual savings for European businesses. This is a major step towards reaching the burden reduction target of at least 25% and at least 35% for SMEs. Moreover, with these measures, the Commission also cut additional one-off costs by €4 billion.
The six omnibus packages proposed so far cover:
Sustainability: covering a far-reaching simplification in the fields of sustainability reporting, sustainability due diligence and taxonomy, which will lower costs by €4.5 billion. Additionally, the proposal on the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, adopted in September, removes related obligations for approx. 182,000 – or 90% – of importers, most of which are SMEs, reducing their expenses by €1.2 billion.
Investment simplification: facilitating the deployment and reporting of InvestEU and the European Fund for Strategic Investments, which will cut costs by €350 million, of which €324 million of one-off costs, and mobilise €50 billion in additional public and private investments.
The common agricultural policy: reducing complexity and excessive administrative burden for farmers and national administrations in managing, monitoring and reporting, while leveraging digitalisation to cut annual costs for farmers by €1.58 billion and for administrators by €210 million.
Small mid-caps, digitalisation and common specifications: enabling small mid-cap companies to use the same lighter regulatory requirements as SMEs and removing the need for paper formats in product legislation, which will result in cutting costs by €380 million.
Defence: simplifying EU rules on the European defence industry to facilitate investment, improving funding access and raising readiness to deter major conflict.
Chemicals: cutting compliance costs and administrative burden for the chemical industry, while maintaining a strong level of protection of public health and the environment. The proposal simplifies aspects related to the classification, labelling, and packaging of chemicals, fertilisers and cosmetics, lowering costs by €363 million, of which €290 million are recurrent costs.
How does the Commission identify the areas that need simplification?
The EU is taking a broad approach to make its rules simpler and more effective through:
Implementation Dialogues: meetings that Commissioners hold directly with industry, SMEs, social partners and authorities who are impacted by EU policies. The Commissioners have engaged in 28 dialogues with over 550 stakeholders. These dialogues allowed the Commission to hear directly from practitioners about the challenges they face, good practices and opportunities for simplification in future proposals. The aim is to get concrete feedback on what works for them and what does not and to learn from their practical insights.
Stress Testing: screening EU law to simplify it and make it more cost-effective. The areas for stress-testing are prioritised where burdens are felt most acutely and where there is the greatest potential to simplify. The priorities are drawn up based on stakeholder feedback and analysis, as well as the Draghi report and Letta report.
Reality Checks: workshops with stakeholders who are implementing or directly affected by EU law. Their feedback helps to identify hurdles, as well as successful practices in transposing EU rules at national level, ensuring that the rules deliver the expected benefits. Over 40 reality checks have been carried out or planned. The input from these checks contribute to evaluations and simplification proposals.
What type of implementation support does the Commission provide to Member States to help them implement new policies and regulations?
The Commission is ensuring that Member States have the required support to implement new policies and regulations. To this aim, the Commission launched:
a transposition roadmap IT tool and standardised templates to help Member States transpose EU directives.
a Public Administration Cooperation and Exchange program and the Quality of Public Administration toolbox to build administrative capacity in the Member States. More so, the Just Transition Platform provided technical assistance to 14 Member States; the Smart Specialisation Community of Practice supported 15 Member States, and the Technical Support Instrument for various reforms and key EU policies supported all Member States.
an Interoperable Europe Community, complemented by comprehensive guidelines and the Open-Source Software Catalogue to facilitate interoperability for cross-border digital services.
and a cross-border enforcement operations, together with technical guidance and capacity building to support EU agencies in consistently applying EU law. For example, the European Labour Authority's operations took off in seven Member States.
How does the Commission ensure EU rules are effectively enforced?
The EU's efficacy and benefits of EU laws depend on their full and correct implementation. The Commission cooperates closely with Member States to avoid implementation issues from the outset, but resolute enforcement is necessary when cooperation fails. The Commission has made full use of its enforcement tools to ensure Member States implement jointly agreed rules:
More pre-infringement dialogues: The Commission conducts informal dialogues with Member States to address potential breaches of EU law swiftly and gather information to ascertain whether there has been a breach, with high success rates observed in closed cases (67% in 2025).
Infringement procedures: the Commission can launch formal procedures should cooperation not be sufficient to achieve timely compliance with EU rules. They are effective: so far in 2025, the Commission could close over 400 infringement cases as Member States achieved compliance. This means tangible improvements for our people and businesses, as set out in the report.
Court referrals and financial sanctions: In the first seven months of 2025, the Commission referred 45 cases to the Court of Justice, proposing to impose financial sanctions on the Member State concerned in 15 cases. The Commission has increased the number of referrals to Court in recent years to ensure that EU law is enforced fully and correctly.
What are the next steps?
It is up to the European Parliament and the Council to agree on the proposed simplification initiatives.
The EU will continue making EU rules simpler and more straightforward to implement, with a clear emphasis on simplification proposals. To that aim, more than half of the initiatives announced in the Commission's Work programme for 2026 are simplification proposals.
All Commissioners will continue engaging with stakeholders through implementation dialogues and with the European Parliament and the Council through the Annual Progress Reports on Simplification, Implementation and Enforcement.
The Commission will also keep developing targeted support tools and reinforce capacity-building programs for Member States to ensure they have the resources needed for successful implementation.
