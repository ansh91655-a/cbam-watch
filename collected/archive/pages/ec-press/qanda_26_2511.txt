[제목] Questions and answers on the outcomes of the 2026 EU-UK Summit

SECOND EU-UK SUMMIT
What has been agreed at today's Summit?
A Joint EU-UK Statement has issued from today's Summit.
It builds upon the outputs of the 2025 Summit which remain valid:
the EU-United Kingdom Joint Statement 2025;
the Security and Defence Partnership between the EU and the United Kingdom; and
the Common Understanding, a political document agreed between the European Commission and the United Kingdom.
In the Common Understanding, the European Commission and the United Kingdom committed to explore a number of areas where there was potential to strengthen cooperation between the EU and the United Kingdom.
Today's Summit Joint Statement welcomes the progress made in the delivery of the commitments from the Common Understanding and identifies new areas for cooperation.
How do the EU-UK Summits relate to the existing EU-UK agreements?
The Withdrawal Agreement, including the Windsor Framework, the Trade and Cooperation Agreement and the more recent Agreement on Gibraltar remain the cornerstone of the EU-United Kingdom relationship. They provide a solid foundation for our cooperation across a broad range of areas.
At the 2026 Summit, the EU and the United Kingdom have renewed their commitment to the full, faithful and timely implementation of these agreements.
Today's Summit builds upon this solid foundation.
Withdrawal Agreement
The Withdrawal Agreement, together with the Trade and Cooperation Agreement and the more recent Agreement on Gibraltar constitute the foundation of the EU-UK bilateral relationship.
Their full, timely and faithful implementation is key.
The Withdrawal Agreement is important, as, inter alia:
It protects millions of EU citizens in the United Kingdom and UK nationals in the EU, guaranteeing their rights to live, work and study in their host States, now and in the future;
It includes the Windsor Framework, which ensures the avoidance of a hard border on the island of Ireland, while protecting the EU Single Market, to which Northern Ireland has a unique access.
The Framework continues to provide benefits for people and businesses in Northern Ireland across areas, ranging from agri-foods to medicines, trade and VAT and excise, to regular systematic engagement with Northern Ireland stakeholders.
It provides the arrangements between the EU and the United Kingdom in relation to the Sovereign Base Areas of the United Kingdom in Cyprus, in areas such as customs, taxation, agriculture, SPS and fisheries.
It established joint governance bodies for the implementation of the Agreement, which meet on a regular basis and advance the work for the benefit of both the EU and the United Kingdom.
The agenda, decisions, joint declarations and joint statements of the Withdrawal Agreement Joint Committee are published on the website of the Commission.
Has the joint review envisioned under Article 776 Trade and Cooperation Agreement taken place?
The Trade and Cooperation Agreement is the most ambitious agreement of its kind that the EU has concluded with a third country.
It has been in operation for five and a half years and is functioning smoothly. The 2025 and 2026 Summit outcomes demonstrate how the TCA can be further developed and built upon.
Article 776 of the TCA is a commitment to jointly review the functioning of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement, five years after its entry into force.
The Summit Joint Statement affirms the EU and UK's shared view that the TCA functions effectively, though it notes that certain barriers persist and require further efforts.
The EU and UK jointly review the functioning of the TCA at the regular meetings of the joint bodies whose role it is to monitor and review the implementation of the TCA and to assist the Partnership Council in the performance of its tasks.
The annual meetings discuss ongoing issues in wide-ranging areas such as fisheries, law enforcement, energy and cooperation on competition. The agendas and reports of these meetings are available on the websites of the Commission.
The Commission's reports on the implementation of the TCA are published regularly. The Commission's fifth annual report to the European Parliament and the Council on the implementation of the TCA was published in April 2026.
PEACE, SECURITY AND DEFENCE, DEVELOPMENT, HEALTH SECURITY
Ukraine Support Loan
The European Union and the United Kingdom stand firm in their support of Ukraine and our commitment to support Ukraine as long as it takes to bring Russia's illegal war of aggression to an end and secure a comprehensive, just and lasting peace.
The Joint Statement welcomed the UK's participation under the €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan since July 2026.
This is an important step allowing Ukraine to procure, under the Ukraine Support Loan, from a larger pool of defence manufacturers, ensuring they have the capabilities to withstand Russian aggression. The UK will provide a fair and proportionate contribution to the costs arising from borrowing, commensurate with the value of contracts awarded to UK companies.
The loan is providing Ukraine with the predictable financial support it needs, both for budget support and defence spending, over 2026-2027.
What cooperation is envisaged on defence industrial issues?
Given the global security context, the EU and the UK have announced their intention to enhance exchanges on defence industrial issues, taking into account the objective of developing resilient European supply chains and capabilities and addressing critical capability gaps, including through the Capability Coalitions.
What has been agreed in respect of Democratic Resilience?
Having identified the potential for further cooperation on broader democratic resilience, and in line with the whole-of-society approach under the European Democracy Shield, the EU and the UK have agreed to develop a dialogue on Democratic Resilience.
The dialogue will strengthen the resilience of democratic institutions, safeguard the integrity of the information space, supporting electoral integrity, as well as supporting relevant stakeholders, including civil society, to increase capacity to defend common democratic principles, and boosting citizens' engagement and societal resilience. This will include the sharing of information and best practices in these areas.
EU-United Kingdom cooperation on democracy has so far mainly focused on countering foreign information manipulation and interference, for example through the EU-United Kingdom Security and Defence Partnership agreed at the first EU-United Kingdom Summit. Through the Partnership, strategic consultations have been set up on hybrid threats including countering foreign information manipulation and interference.
What has been agreed today in respect of health security?
Both the Trade and Cooperation Agreement (Article 702) and the Common Understanding (paragraph 11) provide for cooperation in the area of health security.
Following negotiations held between November 2025 and October 2026 and the completion of the respective internal procedures, today the EU and UK welcomed the imminent signature of a Memorandum of Understanding in the area of health security, including matters concerning medical countermeasures.
The EU and the United Kingdom are also discussing the establishment of a Specialised Committee on Health Security.
PUTTING PEOPLE AT THE CENTRE OF THE EU-UK RELATIONSHIP
What has been agreed on Erasmus+?
The Common Understanding invited the European Commission and the United Kingdom to work towards the association of the United Kingdom to the European Union Erasmus+ programme. Following the negotiations, on 15 April 2026 the UK's participation in Erasmus+ in 2027 was enabled.
In the 2026 Summit Joint Statement the EU and the United Kingdom underlined the significance of the United Kingdom's participation in Erasmus+ in 2027 and agreed to explore opportunities beyond 2027.
The association of the United Kingdom to Erasmus+ will support activities such as exchanges for students, at university and other academic levels, exchanges and training activities for academic staff, cooperation among higher education institutions, youth exchanges, or the support for the professional development of sport staff.
What has been agreed as regards a youth experience scheme?
In the Common Understanding, the European Commission and the United Kingdom agreed to work towards a balanced youth experience scheme The 2026 Summit welcomed the conclusion of negotiations on the agreement that will allow 100,000 young people from the EU and the UK respectively to reside for up to three years in each other's territories to engage in activities such as work, studies, au-pairing, volunteering, or simply travelling.
Does the youth experience scheme reinstate free movement as it applied while the United Kingdom was a Member State?
The Youth Experience Scheme is not about free movement of persons.
The scheme provides for age-limited (18-31), time-limited (up to three years) mobility, subject to the fulfilment of certain conditions to be checked before the mobility can take place. The conditions would also need to be met during the stay.
Are lower tuition fees for EU students in the UK part of the YES agreement?
The aim of the YES is to allow young people from the EU and the UK to reside for up to three years to engage in activities such as work, studies, au-pairing, volunteering, or simply travelling. The EU and the UK share the objective of further increasing opportunities for studying.
This is why the EU and the UK have announced their intention to work towards a supplementing agreement to the Trade and Cooperation Agreement on tuition fees for EU students in the UK and for UK students in the EU.
The agreement will apply to all students irrespective of the immigration pathway.
It was agreed at the Summit to conclude the negotiations on the agreement within six months from the date of the Summit.
Why has it been so difficult to resolve issues related to touring artists since Brexit?
Touring artists are not covered by the Trade and Cooperation Agreement commitments on service suppliers.
Consequently, United Kingdom touring artists are subject to Schengen rules, customs and VAT obligations, as well as the rules on cabotage.
The Common Understanding recognises the value of travel and cultural and artistic exchanges, including the activities of touring artists.
Close coordination and continued engagement remain essential to ensuring that cultural exchanges can take place effectively in practice.
At the 2026 Summit, the EU and the UK reaffirmed the value of cultural and artistic exchanges, including the activities of touring artists and agreed to explore further mutually beneficial solutions.
STRENGTHENING OUR ECONOMIES WHILE PROTECTING OUR PLANET AND ITS RESOURCES
What cooperation on energy is taking place between the EU and the United Kingdom?
The Trade and Cooperation Agreement provides for cooperation on energy between the EU and the United Kingdom under a dedicated Title.
It sets rules to facilitate trading energy and raw materials across borders, high level principles for the organisation of the gas and electricity markets and promotion of renewables, provides for the cooperation of the EU and UK in terms of network development (especially setting a framework for the UK's interaction with the North Seas Energy Cooperation) and cooperation between the UK transmission system operators and regulators and their European counterparts.
Under the Trade and Cooperation Agreement, the energy title that provided for this cooperation was due to automatically expire on 30 June 2026.
At the 2025 Summit, the European Commission and the United Kingdom reached a political understanding that their cooperation under the Energy Title of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement should be pursued on a continuous basis and that it would thus be appropriate to extend the application of this energy title. They also agreed to proceed with the first of such extensions until 31 March 2027.
The 2026 Summit, welcomed the decision of the Partnership Council to further extend the energy title until 31 March 2028.
How are negotiations going for UK participation in the Union's internal electricity market?
Following the 2025 EU-United Kingdom Summit, the Commission and the United Kingdom held and concluded in December 2025 exploratory discussions to identify the key parameters of an agreement allowing the participation of the United Kingdom in the EU internal electricity market. On that basis, on 30 March 2026 the Council authorised the Commission to negotiate an agreement with the United Kingdom. Negotiations started in May 2026.
The negotiations for an Electricity Agreement are closely linked to the negotiations of a Cohesion Agreement through which the United Kingdom would financially contribute to towards reducing the social, territorial and economic disparities between regions of the EU.
The EU and the United Kingdom have committed in the 2026 Summit Joint Statement to completing these negotiations in 2027, so that all the necessary arrangements take effect as soon as possible.
What has been agreed in respect of UK contribution to cohesion?
Any new agreements giving third countries access to areas of the Union's internal market require a financial contribution towards reducing the social, territorial and economic disparities between regions of the EU.
The negotiations for an Electricity Agreement are the first negotiations where that link will be established in the case of the United Kingdom. At the 2026 Summit, the UK agreed that the agreement on electricity would be linked to a UK financial contribution to reducing, economic, social and territorial disparities between regions of the EU.
This approach is similar to other third countries participating in the Union's internal market, such as Norway or Switzerland.
What work is underway in respect of competition cooperation?
In the Common Understanding, the EU and UK welcomed the conclusions of negotiations for a Competition Cooperation agreement. The Agreement signed on 25 February 2026is a supplementing agreement of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement. Its purpose is to promote cooperation and coordination in competition matters and to enhance the effective enforcement of the EU and UK's competition legislation. It enters into force on 1 January 2027.
What has been agreed as regards fisheries?
At the 2026 Summit, the EU and the United Kingdom noted the decision on full reciprocal access to waters to fish until 30 June 2038.
They reiterated their commitment to work together to ensure sustainability of the fisheries, by reinforcing cooperation, continuing the practice of jointly agreed management measures of shared stocks, and improving the regional planning of measures impacting fishing activities.
In particular, the EU and the United Kingdom will look at how to reverse the declines in key vulnerable species to protect shared fisheries and coastal communities. There was also an agreement to work together collectively with other coastal States to rapidly conclude comprehensive sharing arrangements for stocks of common interest that are managed multilaterally to prevent detrimental effects for our fisheries sectors and coastal communities, as well as to address unsustainable and illegal fishing.
What has been agreed as regards Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) agreement?
In the Common Understanding, the EU and the United Kingdom committed to working towards the establishment of a common sanitary and phytosanitary area through a comprehensive EU- United Kingdom SPS Agreement.
The 2026 Summit welcomed the conclusion of negotiations on an agreement establishing a Common Sanitary and Phytosanitary Area with Certain Common Quality Standards (SPS Agreement).
The agreement would allow for the vast majority of movements of animals, animal products, plants and plant products between Great Britain and the Crown Dependencies and the EU take place without the certificates or controls that are currently required.
The agreement requires that the United Kingdom dynamically aligns to the relevant EU rules. Within an arbitration-based dispute resolution model, the European Court of Justice is the ultimate authority for all questions of European Union law.
What does the termination clause of the SPS agreement say?
In addition to the standard termination provision, the SPS Agreement contains specific provisions on the financial consequences of the termination of the agreement within the first 10 years of its application.
Should the Agreement be terminated by one Party during this period, the other Party will not be left bearing the potentially significant costs of having to re-establish border controls and put the necessary infrastructure and arrangements back in place. The timeframe of 10 years is intended to provide the Parties with sufficient stability and predictability to reap the benefits of the Agreement.
The provision is reciprocal and includes safeguards to ensure fairness and proportionality. Any costs recoverable would be determined by common accord between the Parties.
In the absence of agreement, the possibility of referring the matter to an arbitration tribunal would provide an independent mechanism for resolving disputes.
The approach therefore seeks to balance the right of either Party to terminate the Agreement with the need to ensure that the financial consequences of such termination are addressed fairly.
How would the SPS Agreement affect the Windsor Framework?
The SPS Agreement will remove the vast majority of SPS certificates and checks for the movements of animals, animal products, plants and plant products between Great Britain, the Crown Dependencies and the EU.
Through the interaction of the SPS Agreement and the Windsor Framework, these benefits would also be extended to the same movements between Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
Other flexibilities currently covered by the Windsor Framework SPS arrangements, which will not be in scope of the SPS Agreement, would continue to be available for Northern Ireland contingent upon existing safeguards for the protection of the EU market.
What has been agreed as regards linking of emissions trading systems?
In the Common Understanding, the EU and the United Kingdom committed to working towards establishing a link between their respective carbon markets: the United Kingdom Emission Trading Scheme and the European Union Emission Trading System.
To achieve this, the United Kingdom committed to dynamic alignment with the relevant EU rules. The 2026 Summit welcomed the conclusion of negotiations on an agreement on linking the emissions trading systems.
The ETS agreement will establish a link between the United Kingdom and the EU emission trading systems, while they remain separate. It includes an obligation to recognise emission allowances under the emissions trading system of one party under the emissions trading system of the other party.
This means that an EU company will be able to purchase an allowance issued in the United Kingdom and surrender it in the EU to satisfy its obligations under EU law. The same possibility will exist for United Kingdom companies. Furthermore, the agreement will provide the basis for a possible future mutual exemption from the EU and United Kingdom carbon border adjustment mechanisms.
The UK will expand its ETS, including for aviation and maritime. As a result, over 10 million tonnes of additional CO2 emissions will be included in the system, incentivising decarbonisation. Both EU and UK ETSs will apply monitoring for aviation's non-CO2 climate effects.
Linking the EU and the UK ETSs brings benefits for both sides. The Agreement will ensure that the same set of climate rules related to emissions trading will apply in the EU and the UK, creating a level playing field for businesses on both sides of the Channel.
It will also create a larger, more liquid carbon market with a more stable carbon price, supporting the EU and the UK on our paths to climate neutrality. A larger carbon market also enables more efficient investment decisions, supporting the sustainable development of the EU and UK economies.
What has been agreed as regards financial services under ETS agreement?
In the ETS agreement, the United Kingdom has accepted that its financial rules related to the market for emission allowances and derivatives thereof would be at least as stringent as the EU rules at all times. The ETS agreement provides for a governance structure to exchange on regulatory changes in both jurisdictions and a robust enforcement mechanism.
In addition, the agreement contains provisions on market oversight cooperation between financial regulators.
What work is underway in respect of business mobility?
The Trade and Cooperation Agreement provides for rules on the entry and temporary stay of natural persons for business purposes.
The Common Understanding foresaw the holding of dedicated dialogues on business mobility under the TCA. The European Commission and the United Kingdom held these dialogues in April 2026.
In these dialogues, the Union has repeatedly expressed its concerns with the application of the United Kingdom sponsorship scheme to EU service suppliers going to the United Kingdom to provide a short-term service.
What is the status of the Mutual Recognition Agreement (MRA) on Professional Qualifications in respect of architects?
There is renewed momentum on the proposed mutual recognition of professional qualifications for for architects, as the EU and United Kingdom professional bodies are expected to submit a Joint Recommendation for a Mutual recognition arrangement by the end of 2026.
Negotiations with the United Kingdom could be launched shortly thereafter, and once concluded, the agreement could then be incorporated in the Trade and Cooperation Agreement.
What has been decided in respect of an EU-UK Digital Partnership?
Digital Partnerships are key in the EU's 2025 International Digital Strategy. To date, the EU has concluded Digital Partnerships with Japan and the Republic of Korea (2022), Singapore and Canada (2023) and Brazil (2026).
The EU and United Kingdom announced their intention to launch such a partnership early next year in the 2026 Summit Joint Statement.
An EU-United Kingdom Digital Partnership will enhance cooperation on digital and technology matters, in areas that could cover secure and resilient connectivity, artificial intelligence, including AI security and safety, critical technologies, including quantum, digital wallets and credentials, trust services, online safety and multilateral cooperation to deepen practical cooperation and to bring actions under a common structure.
This will help us drive innovation, strengthen trade, and benefit businesses and consumers alike.
INTERNAL SECURITY AND JUDICIAL COOPERATION
What has been agreed in the area of internal security and judicial cooperation?
Already today, the Trade and Cooperation Agreement is a very good basis for law enforcement and judicial cooperation for the authorities of the EU Member States and the United Kingdom.
The United Kingdom is the closest partner in this area with the deepest cooperation, asides from the Schengen-associated countries.
Current cooperation covers the exchange of DNA, fingerprints and vehicle registration data; transfer and processing of passenger name record data; cooperation on operational information; cooperation with Europol (the United Kingdom has 20 liaison officers at Europol); cooperation with Eurojust (the United Kingdom has a liaison prosecutor posted at Eurojust); surrender; mutual legal assistance; the exchange of criminal record information; anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing; judicial cooperation on asset freezing and confiscation; as well as a counter-terrorism dialogue.
Since the last summit, a working arrangement on tackling the challenges related to drug trafficking and drug use was added, as well as a working arrangement on the United Kingdom's liaison officer at Europol.
Automated searching of vehicle registration data between the EU and the United Kingdom started on 15 June 2026. This will render the work of law enforcement more effective across borders.
At the 2026 Summit, the EU and the United Kingdom have agreed to even more cooperation.
Continue the efforts to reinforce cooperation under the existing agreements, in particular at Europol and Eurojust.
Closely coordinate on the removal of convicted foreign national offenders of the EU and the United Kingdom to their home country;
Continue to explore ways to improve the exchange of personal data for law enforcement, judicial cooperation and border management purposes, such as facial images and criminal record information.
IRREGULAR MIGRATION
What has been agreed today on irregular migration?
In the Common Understanding, the European Commission and the United Kingdom agreed to deepen cooperation on the challenges posed by irregular migration.
The Joint Statement welcomed deepened cooperation between the European Commission and the United Kingdom on this issue.
The implementation of these commitments has progressed across several strands, including upstream migration cooperation, cooperation with Frontex and the EU Agency for Asylum and cooperation in other fora such as the European Migration Network and the Global Alliance to Counter Migrant Smuggling.
In June 2026, the Commission adopted an Action Plan for the Channel Migratory Route, providing a more structured framework for cooperation. Its implementation requires coordinated EU, Member State and United Kingdom action.
Is the UK joining the EU's Channel Action Plan?
The European Commission's Channel Action Plan was adopted on 16 June 2026. It provides a framework for cooperation to tackle irregular migration and the criminal networks facilitating it.
The 2026 Joint Statement encourages the European Union and the United Kingdom to work closely on joint activities under the Action Plan, including through coordinated action against migrant smuggling, addressing the factors driving irregular migration and strengthening cooperation along key routes of irregular migration towards the EU and the UK.
The European Union and the United Kingdom also agreed to establish a Structured Dialogue on migration covering in particular the joint activities under the Channel Action Plan.
Given the shared nature of these challenges, any new joint activities should be underpinned by a joint commitment, including, where appropriate, financial contributions, reflecting the shared roles and responsibilities for them.
BUILDING A CLOSER MORE RESILIENT PARTNERSHIP
What is the closer, more resilient partnership being built today?
There is a shared interest between the EU and the UK to build an even closer partnership.
The partnership for economic resilience would allow the addressing of economic dependencies by developing a common prosperity and economic security space, with the principles of trust at its core.
Through this coordination the EU and the UK commit, on the basis of reciprocity, to strive to limit negative effects on one another in their measures impacting specific supply chains.
Under this partnership, where the EU and the UK agree to cooperate regarding a particular supply chain, the UK would ensure outcomes consistent with the relevant EU measures in order to ensure that the supply chain in question is protected in a consistent way across the EU and UK markets and guard against potential risks of circumvention.
What has been decided today on economic security?
The Joint Statement agreed to establish a Dialogue on Economic Security to coordinate our economic security policies.
Under this dialogue, the EU and the UK will cooperate on export controls, on countering technology leakage in critical sectors, on mitigating risks to critical infrastructure, on resilient supply chains, including critical raw materials, tackling vulnerabilities and building on the complementarities of our economies, including early warning for supply disruptions.
The objective of this coordination is to ensure a more effective and efficient response to common challenges and ensure policy alignment, for example by avoiding the circumvention of the measures we put in place to ensure the resilience of our economies.
