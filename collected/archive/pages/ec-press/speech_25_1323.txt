[제목] Remarks by Commissioner Hoekstra in the debate on simplifying and strengthening the carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) in the plenary of the European Parliament

Thank you very much Mr President.
Many thanks to the Rapporteur for his eloquent speech and his wise words.
Let me briefly recap the logic of CBAM and where we stand today. I'm saying that under the guidance of one of its intellectual fathers being in the room, and this House having put much effort into making sure CBAM would see the light of day.
CBAM is a necessary and very effective add-on to our ETS system. It ensures we prevent carbon leaking from the EU because that would be bad for everyone – clearly for climate but it would also be to the detriment of our businesses and consumers.
Having said, we also want to make sure that our CBAM is as effective as possible – and this is why we're currently looking into a set of four different types of improvements.
The first one is making sure that we don't see any circumvention to the system as we have built it.
The second one is scope.
Third is exports, and making sure our exporters don't face unfair competition on the world market.
And fourth and finally – and that is the reason Chair we're here today – is simplification.
Let's be honest. The Rapporteur was already referring to the numbers. It's quite rare that you manage to uphold the content, the position to a 99% degree, and yet make sure you cut bureaucracy by 90%. We're taking 90% of all companies out of scope while upholding 99% of the climate logic.
It rarely happens but we have it here in CBAM. It's a necessary adjustment that does justice to our joint mission – bridging competitiveness with climate policy and making our Union more independent.
Let me close, dear Chair, and thank your esteemed Parliament and the Members present in particular for the time and effort they have invested in this specific part of adjustments. We'll deal with the other parts hopefully later on this year given how critically important they will be for climate policy and competitiveness.
