Verification of CBAM emissions
Information on CBAM verification rules and accreditation framework
PlayPause
Under CBAM, importers must submit annual declarations based either on actual verified emissions data or on default emissions values. In the case of actual emissions, the verification must be carried out by independent verifiers accredited by EU national accreditation bodies. This ensures the credibility, consistency and transparency of emissions reporting across the EU
Page contents
Page contents
Why verification matters?
Robust verification of the actual emissions data provided by non-EU operators is essential to the proper functioning of CBAM. It ensures that the emissions data used by importers is trustworthy and comparable across operators and sectors.
CBAM verifiers provide reasonable assurance that the calculation of embedded emissions is correct. In turn, verifiers are subject to strict oversight from EU countries’ national accreditation bodies. Verification reports used by importers may also be subject to a review by the European Commission and the National Competent Authorities.
Who can be a verifier?
To become a CBAM verifier, applicants must receive CBAM accreditation provided by EU countries’ national accreditation bodies. Applications to become a verifier are open to any company which can demonstrate that they:
have the required technical competence in the relevant industrial sectors
operate independently and impartially
apply consistent and reliable verification approaches
The first CBAM verifiers are expected to receive CBAM accreditation around September 2026. The list of accredited CBAM verifiers will be published on this page. CBAM verifiers have the obligation of registering in the CBAM registry.

National Accreditation Bodies
National accreditation bodies (NABs) located in an EU country (or an EEA country, after the incorporation of the CBAM Regulation in the EEA Agreement) are the sole stakeholders competent to grant accreditation for the CBAM.
As a general rule, verification companies established in an EU or EEA country must seek CBAM accreditation in the NAB of their country of establishment. Only if this country does not offer the relevant accreditation services can applicants apply with another NAB. Verification companies that are established outside the EU may apply in any national accreditation body offering this service.
Building on the information provided by the European cooperation for Accreditation (EA), Commission services maintain a list of NABs that provide CBAM accreditation services.

Verification of non-EU installation operators
CBAM verification applies at the level of the installation where CBAM goods are produced.
National Accreditation Bodies
National Accreditation Bodies (NABs) ensure that CBAM verifiers are qualified to verify embedded emissions by accrediting them for CBAM activities and providing oversight throughout the year.
Non-EU installation operators
Non-EU installation operators monitor and calculate embedded emissions in accordance with the CBAM methodology and provide the relevant information to an accredited verifier.
Accredited CBAM verifiers
Accredited CBAM verifiers review the monitoring approach, emissions calculations and supporting evidence. Following the assessment, they issue a verification report which can be included to the CBAM declaration.
CBAM declarants
CBAM declarants retrieve the actual verified emissions from the CBAM Registry and use this information to complete and submit their CBAM declaration.

The upper row begins with the European Co-operation for Accreditation (EA), which performs peer evaluations of Member States’ national accreditation bodies.
