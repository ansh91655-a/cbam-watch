CBAM: Commission adopts rules on the recognition of carbon prices paid in non-EU countries
CBAM: Commission adopts rules on the recognition of carbon prices paid in non-EU countries
News article
16 November 2026
Directorate-General for Taxation and Customs Union
4 min read
Today, the Commission published Implementing Regulation 2026/2498 setting out detailed rules under the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) for the deduction of carbon prices effectively paid in non-EU countries. The rules were refined following extensive input from stakeholders.
Where operators in a third country have already paid a carbon price, it is taken into account and deducted from the corresponding CBAM obligation based on rules set in the Implementing Regulation. By clarifying these rules, the Commission ensures CBAM as a fair mechanism that avoids double charging and reflects the carbon price already paid on the embedded emissions of imported goods.
The Implementing Regulation specifies how such deductions can be claimed, the evidence required to make a claim, how that evidence must be certified by an independent person accredited by a national accreditation body, how the carbon price paid is attributed to the embedded emissions of the declared goods, and how it is converted into a reduction in the number of CBAM certificates to be surrendered.
Eligible carbon pricing mechanisms
A carbon price paid in a non-EU country may take the form of an emissions trading system, a carbon tax, a levy or a fee. To be eligible under CBAM, this mechanism must be binding and impose compliance obligations on all operators active in the relevant sectors. The scheme must apply the same pricing obligation to the emissions of all goods produced by the respective manufacturer, whether they are for domestic sale or are exported.
To give stakeholders greater clarity, the Implementing Regulation includes a non-exhaustive list of emissions trading systems and carbon taxes that meet the definitions set in the CBAM Regulation. Absence from the list does not imply that a mechanism is ineligible. The Commission will update the list regularly, also based on requests and information from third countries, as new schemes are developed.
Compliance options and carbon credits
The evidence of the carbon price effectively paid under a carbon price mechanism can cover all forms of compliance options allowed by third-country authorities, including carbon credits, within the limits and conditions of the relevant carbon pricing mechanism. Most compliance schemes allow for at least part of the obligation to be met with the purchase of carbon credits, either on domestic or international markets.
When international carbon credits are used, only the price paid for credits authorised and issued under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement will be taken into account. This is to safeguard the environmental integrity of CBAM and to promote common global standards. The use of such international carbon credits is however limited to a maximum of 10% of the reported emissions under the carbon pricing mechanism to ensure that most decarbonisation efforts are indeed pursued domestically, encouraging producers to reduce their own emissions by developing and using the most efficient technologies.
Transitional arrangement for 2026 and 2027
To introduce the system smoothly, accredited CBAM verifiers will, for the first two years, be able to certify evidence of the carbon price already effectively paid in a non-EU country, without needing extra accreditation from an EU national accreditation body. This means the same verifier can check both the embedded emissions in the goods and the carbon price already paid. As a result, declarants can claim the deduction from the start.
From 2028 onwards, the person certifying the carbon price paid will need a specific accreditation granted by an EU national accreditation body, as is already the case for the verification of embedded emissions. Existing verifiers will accordingly need to extend the scope of their accreditation, if they also want to certify the carbon price paid in a third country.
Default carbon prices
As an alternative to certified evidence, declarants may claim the deduction based on the relevant default carbon prices. Introduced as a major CBAM simplification in 2025, this option reduces administrative burden and compliance costs for third-country operators and declarants.
The Commission will publish both the methodology used to determine default carbon prices and the values available in the CBAM Registry. These will be provided for all countries with national carbon pricing mechanisms, so that they can be used in the first CBAM declarations, due by 30 September 2027.
Guidance and review
In the coming months, the Commission will publish guidance documents and templates to support third-country operators, certifiers and CBAM declarants. It will also review the application of the new rules before the end of 2027, drawing on the experience and lessons learnt from the first CBAM declarations.
