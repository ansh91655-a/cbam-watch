18Nov2026
Extension of CBAM to Iceland and Norway
EEA Joint Committee Decision No 283/2026, which extends CBAM to Iceland and Norway, entered into force on 18 November 2026. From 1 January 2027, the same CBAM rules will apply to imports of covered goods into Iceland and Norway as to imports into the EU.
22Oct2026
New guidance on electricity imports under Centralised Clearance for Import
The European Commission has published a Guidance for import of Electricity under the Centralised Clearance for Import (CCI) framework to support a harmonised approach across the EU. It covers authorisation, declaration and supervision measures under CCI and Entry in the Declarant’s Records (EIDR), as well as CBAM requirements and compliance obligations for electricity imports.
05Oct2026
Q3 2026 CBAM Certificate price now available!
Each 2026 quarterly price is calculated by the Commission in the first calendar week after the quarter ends, based on publicly available EU ETS auction information.
30Sep2026
Webinar follow-up: “CBAM from policy to practice – emissions calculation, declarations, and certificates”
The European Commission held the fourth in a series of webinars on the implementation of the EU’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The webinar covers the policy context and practical steps for the first compliance deadline of 30 September 2027, when the first CBAM declarations and corresponding certificates are due.

CBAM - Do you import any of these products into the EU? (updated)
From 1 January 2026, EU importers - or their indirect customs representatives - importing more than the single mass-based threshold of 50 tonnes of CBAM goods into the EU need a CBAM account number or application reference number under the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) will be extended to Iceland and Norway following the entry into force of the European Economic Area (EEA) Joint Committee Decision No 283/2026 on 18 November 2026. As of 1 January 2027, CBAM will apply in Norway and Iceland to goods imported into their customs territories, in the same way that it applies to goods entering the EU customs territory

How CBAM certificates work
Starting in 2027, CBAM importers must submit their annual CBAM declaration by 30 September for imports of CBAM goods during the preceding year.
The CBAM declaration reports the quantity of goods imported, their embedded emissions, and the corresponding number of certificates.

CBAM - Actual vs default values in your declaration
CBAM requires EU importers of certain goods to become authorised CBAM declarants, report the embedded carbon emissions in these goods, and meet related financial obligations. By 30 September 2027, importers of CBAM goods must submit their first CBAM declaration - covering embedded emissions from 2026 imports - and surrender the corresponding CBAM certificates. Importers can report emissions using default values provided by the Commission or actual values. If an importer chooses to report actual values, the producer in the third country must provide verified emissions data on the total embedded emissions.
